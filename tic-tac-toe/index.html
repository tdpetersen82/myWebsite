How to play Tic-Tac-Toe

Tic-Tac-Toe (also called noughts and crosses, or Xs and Os) is the classic three-in-a-row game on a 3×3 grid. Tap a square to place your X, and get three in a row — across, down, or diagonally — before the computer does. Play against the computer on three levels, or switch to 2 players and pass the screen to a friend.

Rules

  • You are X, the computer is O. X starts the very first round.
  • Take turns placing a mark in any empty square.
  • Three in a row wins — across, down, or diagonally.
  • All nine squares filled with no three-in-a-row is a tie.
  • The loser starts the next round. After a tie, the starting player swaps.
  • Stats save as you play. Ties don't break your win streak — a loss does.

Controls

  • Tap or click an empty square — place your mark
  • 1–9 — place a mark with the keyboard (1 is top-left, 9 is bottom-right)
  • Easy Medium Hard — pick the computer's level
  • 2 players — pass-and-play with a friend on one screen
  • New game — start the round over

Tips & strategy

  • Take the center. The middle square touches four winning lines — more than any other square. If you start, start there.
  • Corners over edges. Each corner sits on three lines; each edge only two. If the center is gone, grab a corner.
  • Block first, then build. Before placing your mark, check whether the other player has two in a row. Stop it now or lose next turn.
  • Set up a fork. The only way to beat a careful opponent is two threats at once — two lines that each need just one more mark. They can only block one.
  • Watch the diagonals. The sneakiest wins run corner to corner. If your opponent holds two opposite corners, play carefully.

About Tic-Tac-Toe

Tic-tac-toe is one of the oldest games still played — the ancient Romans scratched a three-in-a-row game called terni lapilli onto stones and floors, and "noughts and crosses" has filled the margins of school notebooks for centuries. In 1952 a tic-tac-toe program called OXO became one of the first computer games ever written. The game is also famously solved: when both players make their best moves, every round ends in a tie — which is exactly how the Hard level here plays.
