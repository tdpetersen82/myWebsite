Make your own crossword puzzle — free

This crossword maker turns a list of your own words into a real, interlocking crossword in one click. Type each word on its own line — add a clue after a colon if you like — and the generator weaves the words together criss-cross style, numbers the grid, and builds the Across and Down clue lists. Solve it right on the page, send it to a friend as a single link, or print it out with an answer key on the second page. No signup, no downloads, and nothing is uploaded — the whole puzzle lives in your browser.

How to make a crossword

  • Enter your words. One per line, 2–20 words, each 2–15 letters. Write WORD: clue text to add a clue, or just the word by itself.
  • Build it. The generator places the longest word first, then tries every legal crossing for each remaining word, favoring layouts with more crossings and a compact shape. If a word shares no letters with the rest, it's left out and you'll see a note.
  • Play it. Click a cell and type; click the cell again to switch between Across and Down. Use Check to mark wrong letters, or Reveal if you're stuck.
  • Share or print it. Copy link packs the entire puzzle into the web address. Print puzzle produces a clean black-and-white page plus an answer key.

Tips for a good crossword

  • Pick words that share letters. Crossings are what hold a crossword together. Common letters — E, A, R, S, T, O — give the generator many places to interlock.
  • Mix word lengths. A few long anchors (8–12 letters) plus a handful of short words weave together far better than ten words of the same length.
  • 8–14 words is the sweet spot. Enough for a satisfying grid, few enough that everything usually fits.
  • Theme it. Birthday facts, vocabulary lists, wedding trivia, a science unit — puzzles about one topic are more fun to solve and easier to clue.
  • Write clues that hint, not spell. "Planet famous for its rings" beats "starts with S". If you skip clues entirely, solvers get an alphabetical word bank instead, fit-the-words style.

Printable crosswords with an answer key

The print layout is built for paper: page one holds the title, the empty numbered grid, and the clue lists (or the word bank), all in plain black and white with no site chrome. Page two is the answer key with every letter filled in — hand out page one, keep page two. It works for classroom worksheets, party games, baby showers, or a puzzle tucked into a card.
