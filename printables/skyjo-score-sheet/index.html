The rules that get disputed

  • Start: 12 cards in a 3×4 grid, flip any 2
  • Turn: draw or take the discard — swap it in, or discard and flip one of yours
  • Three equal cards in a column — the column is discarded (counts 0)
  • Flipping your last card ends the round — everyone else gets one more turn
  • The finisher must have the strictly lowest round score — otherwise their round score doubles (a tie doubles too; only positive scores double)

How to play Skyjo

Everyone tends a 3×4 grid of face-down cards worth −2 to 12, revealed and swapped one decision at a time. On your turn, draw from the deck or take the discard, then either swap it for any card in your grid (face-up or face-down) or — if you drew and don't want it — discard it and flip one of your own. Low cards are treasure, high cards are debt, and three equal cards stacked in a column vanish entirely.

Ending a round is a threat, not a victory lap

The moment someone's twelfth card is face-up, everyone else gets exactly one more turn and the round scores. Here's the famous part: the finisher must end with the strictly lowest score, or their round score doubles — and a tie triggers it too. Racing to finish with a mediocre grid is how a 14-point round becomes 28. The pad prints the rule verbatim because every table relitigates it.

Reading the pad

Each player gets a round column and a running total. The game has a hard ceiling, not a fixed length: play until someone's total touches 100, then the lowest total wins — so the pad's totals column is the whole drama.

Frequently asked

Do negative scores double? No — the doubling penalty applies only when the finisher's round score is positive.

How many rounds does a game take? Usually six to nine with typical scores; the sheet's ten rows cover a full game with room to spare.
