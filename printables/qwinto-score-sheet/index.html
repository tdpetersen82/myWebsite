How the Qwinto sheet works

Qwinto is a roll-and-write: on each turn the roller picks one, two or all three coloured dice, rolls (with one optional re-roll of the same dice), and calls the sum. Everyone may then write that sum in one field — but only in a row whose colour matches one of the dice rolled.

The two placement rules

Within a row, numbers must ascend from left to right, with no repeats — gaps are fine and can be filled later. Within a column, no number may repeat, and that includes the two-field columns, not just the three-field ones. Each row also has one missing field that stays empty; it is part of the board, not a misprint.

Pentagons

Five columns on the board are three fields tall, and each contains one pentagon. Fill all three fields of such a column and the number written in its pentagon scores again as bonus points at the end. Placing a big number in a pentagon is how large scores happen.

Failed attempts

If the player who rolled cannot — or chooses not to — enter the sum, they mark one failed-attempt box: −5 points each. Only the roller ever takes a failed attempt; everyone else simply passes.

Ending and scoring

The game ends the moment someone completes two full rows, or marks their fourth failed attempt. Score each row: complete rows score their rightmost number, incomplete rows score one point per entry. Add each filled column's pentagon number, subtract five per failed attempt, and the highest total wins.

Frequently asked

Can I write in any row? Only a row matching the colour of a die you actually rolled — rolling fewer dice narrows your options but makes the sum easier to place.

What are these sheets for? Replacement boards for people who own the game and have run through the pad. You still need the game's three dice.
