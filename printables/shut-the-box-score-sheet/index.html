The rules

  • Roll two dice, then flip down any set of open tiles summing exactly to the roll
  • When the open tiles total 6 or less, you may roll one die (common rule — agree first)
  • Can’t match the roll? Stuck — your round score is the sum of tiles still open
  • Flip every tile and you’ve shut the box: score 0 (many tables call it an instant win)
  • Lowest total after the agreed rounds wins
Boxes come with tiles 1–9 (traditional), 1–10 and 1–12 — the score grid works for all of them. The one-die option and instant-win-on-shut are the two rules tables differ on most; settle both before the first roll.

How to play Shut the Box

A row of numbered tiles stands open; two dice try to knock them down. Each roll, flip any set of open tiles that sums exactly to your dice — roll an 8 and you might close the 8, or 6+2, or 5+2+1. Keep rolling until nothing fits. The tiles left standing are your score for the round, and in this game golf rules apply: low is good.

The choices that matter

Every flip is a tiny puzzle: closing big tiles early protects you from big leftovers, but keeping small tiles flexible keeps future rolls alive. The one-die option — allowed once your open tiles total six or less — is where endgames are won, turning a hopeless 2-and-1 position into a coin flip.

Scoring an evening

The sheet runs two games of eight rounds for up to six players, with running totals — or play the elimination way, where anyone passing 45 drops out. And a shut box (every tile down) scores zero at minimum; half the world's pubs call it an instant win instead, so the sheet makes you agree first.

Frequently asked

Do you have to use both dice? Yes — the flip must match the full sum of the roll (the one-die rule is the only exception).

Can you split a roll across turns? No — each roll's flip happens entirely on that roll, and a roll you can't use ends your round.
