One deal, three games

  • Ante: every player, 1 chip on each of the nine sections; deal ALL cards + one spare hand (uneven hands are fine)
  • 1 · Hearts payout: holders of the marked hearts (and 8-9-10 in one suit) collect those sections
  • 2 · Poker: everyone picks their best five cards; bet into POT, best hand takes it
  • 3 · Michigan: dealer leads their lowest card; play climbs that suit in sequence, aces high; on a stop, the last player restarts in a different suit (many editions: opposite color)
  • First out of cards takes KITTY + 1 chip per card left from every player
  • Unclaimed sections (nobody held the card) carry over — the pots grow every deal

How to play Tripoley on this mat

Tripoley is three small games stacked into one deal — a hearts payout, a hand of poker, and a race of Michigan Rummy — glued together by the chip sections on the mat. Print the mat, drop it in the middle of the table, and ante one chip to each section. Deal out the whole deck plus one spare hand, face down; uneven hands are part of the game, and the dealer may swap their hand for the spare sight unseen.

Phase by phase

Hearts payout: holders of the marked hearts show them and sweep those sections — the K-Q section needs both cards in one hand, and 8-9-10 pays for the run in any single suit. Poker: everyone chooses five cards from their hand, a round of betting goes into the Pot, best hand takes it. Michigan: the dealer leads their lowest card, sequences climb until they stop and restart in a new suit, and the first empty hand takes the Kitty plus a chip per card from every straggler.

Why the pots get silly

Any section nobody could claim — an ace of hearts asleep in the spare hand — carries its chips forward, and everyone antes onto it again next deal. Three deals of an unclaimed K-Q builds the kind of pot that makes grown relatives gasp over a card game, which is the entire point of Tripoley. Warm up the poker phase with the hand rankings chart, or the stops phase with a few hands of hearts.

Frequently asked

How many can play? Four to nine around one mat, with the deck dealt as far as it goes. Two or three players works but leaves the spare hand holding half the payouts — expect carryovers.

Chips per player? The boxed game starts everyone near fifty; any number works so long as antes stay affordable. Play to a time limit or until one player holds everything, traditionally the same thing.
