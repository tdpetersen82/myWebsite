How to run a trivia night

The machinery is simple: teams get a slip per round, the host reads questions twice, slips come in before answers are revealed, and teams mark each other's. Everything that goes wrong at quiz night — disputed spellings, lost totals, "we definitely handed that in" — is a paperwork problem, which is what this page fixes.

Slips and the grid

The 20-line slip suits a big themed round or a whole short quiz; the 10-line slips are the pub standard. The host grid holds six teams across eight rounds with a running total column — fill it in as marked slips return and the leaderboard maintains itself. Reading scores aloud between rounds is half the entertainment; the grid keeps you honest.

Questions worth stealing

A strong night mixes one anyone-can-get warm-up round, a couple of general rounds, one specialist round (music, sport, maps), and a picture or closest-number round where trailing teams can catch up. Write answers down before the night — adjudicating from memory is how hosts lose friends.

Frequently asked

Half points? Allow them for near-misses if you announce it up front; the slips' score boxes take decimals as happily as integers.

Phones? Face down on the table is the only rule that survives contact with a pub.
