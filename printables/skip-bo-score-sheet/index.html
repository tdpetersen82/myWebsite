Scoring & setup

  • Winner of a game scores 25 + 5 per card left in each opponent’s stock
  • First player to 500 points wins the series
  • Stock piles: 30 cards each (deal 10–15 for faster games)
  • Build piles run 1 → 12; Skip-Bo cards are wild; completed piles recycle
  • Only the stock pile has to empty — hand and discards don’t matter at the buzzer

How to play Skip-Bo

Everyone gets a face-down stock pile with the top card flipped — the only pile that matters. In the middle, up to four building piles grow from 1 to 12, fed from your stock, your five-card hand, and your discard piles; Skip-Bo cards are wild and stand in for anything. Play cards for as long as you can legally build (emptying your hand draws five more), and end your turn with one discard. First stock pile to hit empty wins the game.

The scoring nobody uses — and why it’s good

The official series rules turn casual wins into a race: 25 for the win, plus 5 per card remaining in every opponent's stock, first to 500. Suddenly a blowout is worth more than a squeaker, and grinding your stock down from 29 even in a losing game is defence. The pad gives each player a points column and a running total; fourteen rows is a generous evening.

Playing it well

Stock first, always — every other move exists to unlock the next stock card. Discards are a filing system, not a dumping ground: stack them in runs or repeats you can peel off in order. And holding a Skip-Bo card a beat longer than feels natural is usually right; wilds spent on a 3 are wilds you didn't have for the 12.

Frequently asked

How many can play? Two to six; past four, play partnerships — partners may coach each other’s turns and win together when either stock empties.

What if the draw pile runs dry? Completed building piles are reshuffled into a fresh draw pile — the game never stalls.
