How rubber bridge scoring works

Bridge scoring runs on the line. Points for contract tricks you bid and made go below it — minors at 20, majors at 30, no-trump 40-then-30 — and only those count toward the hundred that makes a game. Everything else lives above the line: overtricks, slam and rubber bonuses, honours, and the penalties you harvest from doubling the opponents into the floor. Win two games and the rubber's yours, with 700 for a clean sweep or 500 if they got one.

Using the pad

Two rubbers fit on the sheet. Start each contract's trick score below the line; when a side crosses 100, rule a fresh line under it — part-scores don't carry into the new game. At the end, add everything, above and below: the side with more points wins the rubber even if they lost it, which happens more often than beginners expect.

The numbers worth memorising

Game needs 100 below: that's 3NT (40+30+30), four of a major (4×30), or five of a minor (5×20) — the shapes every auction bends toward. Slams pay 500/750 small and 1000/1500 grand by vulnerability. And a doubled contract that makes collects a cheeky 50 "for the insult."

Frequently asked

Where do honours go? Above the line, claimed at the end of the hand: 100 for four of the five top trumps in one hand, 150 for all five or four aces at no-trump. Many social games skip them.

What about duplicate scoring? Duplicate uses per-board scores and matchpoints, not a running pad. This sheet is for rubber and Chicago — the kitchen-table games. Hosting more tables than one? The tally cards handle party bridge.
