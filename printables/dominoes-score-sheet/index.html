Block & draw basics

  • Highest double leads; match ends by pip count
  • Can't play? Draw until you can (draw game) or pass (block game)
  • First out wins the round and scores everyone's leftover pips
  • Blocked game — lowest hand wins, scores the difference
  • Play to an agreed total — 100 is common
  • All Fives: also score open ends totalling a multiple of 5, as you play

Scoring dominoes

Straight dominoes — block and draw — scores at the end of each round: the first player to empty their hand wins the round and scores the total pips left in every other hand. This sheet tracks that as a running total per player to an agreed target, with 100 the common game.

Block vs draw

The two classic forms differ only in what happens when you can't play. In the draw game you draw from the boneyard until you can. In the block game you simply pass. Everything else — leading with the highest double, matching ends, scoring — is the same.

Blocked rounds

When nobody can play and the boneyard is empty, the round is blocked: hands are revealed, and the player with the lowest pip count wins the round, scoring the difference between their count and each opponent's (or the total of the others' pips, by house preference — agree first).

All Fives

The popular scoring variant — also called Muggins or Sniff — adds points during play: whenever the open ends of the layout total a multiple of five, the player who made it so scores that total immediately. End-of-round pips still count, usually rounded to the nearest five. If your table plays All Fives, the running columns on this sheet absorb both kinds of score.

Frequently asked

Who goes first? The holder of the highest double leads it. If no double was drawn, reshuffle or lead the heaviest tile by agreement.

What do you play to? 100 is the common target with a double-six set; 150 and 200 suit longer sessions.
