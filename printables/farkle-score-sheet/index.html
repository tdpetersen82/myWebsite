How to use this Farkle score sheet

Farkle is a push-your-luck dice game for two or more players. On your turn you roll six dice, set aside at least one scoring die, and then decide: bank what you have, or re-roll the dice you did not keep and risk it all.

The turn, step by step

  1. Roll all six dice.
  2. Set aside at least one scoring die. If the roll contains no scoring dice at all, you have farkled — your turn ends and you score nothing for it.
  3. Bank your running total, or roll the remaining dice again.
  4. If all six dice end up scoring, you may roll all six again and keep accumulating.

Write the banked total for each turn in that player's column, keeping a running sum. First past 10,000 triggers the final round: everyone else gets one last turn to beat them.

Common house rules worth agreeing before you start

  • Opening score. Many groups require 500 in a single turn before you can start banking. The sheet header assumes this — cross it out if you play open-entry.
  • Three farkles. Some groups deduct 500 or 1,000 after three consecutive farkles.
  • Stealing. In some variants you may pick up the previous player's dice and continue their total.

Farkle strategy

  • With four or more dice left, rolling again is usually right — the chance of farkling is under one in six.
  • With two dice left you farkle about 44% of the time. With one die, two thirds of the time. Bank early rather than chasing.
  • Do not set aside a lone 5 for 50 points if it costs you a die you needed — keeping fewer dice raises your farkle risk sharply.
  • Late in a game, the leader should bank conservatively and the trailer should push.
