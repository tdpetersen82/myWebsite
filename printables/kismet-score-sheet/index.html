How to play Kismet

Kismet is the 1964 answer to the question "what if the dice had suits?" Five dice, up to three rolls a turn keeping what you like, one box filled per turn — familiar bones. The twist is in the pips: 1 and 6 are black, 2 and 5 red, 3 and 4 green, and the card pays for color as well as number.

Reading the card

The Basic section totals each number, and its bonus climbs in three tiers — 35, 55, then 75 at 78-plus, so a hot upper section keeps paying long after the Yahtzee-style bonus would have flatlined. Below, the combination boxes: Straight and Flush pay flat (30 and 35), while Full House, its same-color cousin, 4 of a Kind and Kismet itself pay the dice total plus a bonus — 15, 20, 25 and 50 respectively. Yarborough is the anything-goes box for salvaging a mess.

Where games are won

The color boxes reward planning your keeps by pip color, not just number — holding two red dice chases a Flush and a Full House Same Color at once. And because same-color boxes score dice-plus-bonus, high pips matter: a 6-6-6-1-1 full house in black is worth 35, but 6-6-6-5-5 mixed is only 15 more than its pips. That arithmetic is the game.

Frequently asked

Can a Straight be any five in a row? 1-2-3-4-5 or 2-3-4-5-6, both flat 30.

Is there a second-Kismet bonus? Not in the standard card — unlike Yahtzee, extra five-of-a-kinds have no special payout; roll them into Yarborough or a matching number box.
