How to play Backgammon

Backgammon is a race. Both players move fifteen checkers around a track of twenty-four points toward their own home board, and the first to bring all fifteen home and remove them wins. What makes it a game rather than a dice roll is that you can block and hit your opponent along the way.

Moving

Roll two dice. Each die is a separate move — you may move one checker the total of both, or two different checkers, one per die. Roll doubles and you play that number four times rather than twice, which is why doubles swing games.

You must use both dice if any legal way to do so exists. If only one can be played, you must play the higher.

Points and blots

Two or more checkers on a point make it yours — your opponent cannot land there at all. A single checker is a blot, and if your opponent lands on it, it is hit: it goes to the bar and must re-enter in your opponent's home board before that player may make any other move.

Being hit late in a game is close to fatal, because the checker restarts the entire journey.

Bearing off

Once all fifteen of your checkers are inside your home board — your final six points — you begin bearing off, removing a checker for each die you roll. If you are hit during this phase, you must bring that checker all the way round again before you can resume.

Strategy

Make points, especially in front of your opponent's back checkers. Every point you own is a square they cannot use, and a wall of them slows their whole race.

Build a prime. Six made points in a row cannot be jumped by any roll — a checker trapped behind a full prime cannot move at all until it breaks.

Run when ahead, hold when behind. If you are winning the race, simply race — avoid contact and avoid leaving blots. If you are behind, keep an anchor in your opponent's home board and wait for a shot at one of their blots. Racing when behind rarely works, because you cannot outrun a deficit with average dice.

Bear off evenly. Fill your home points so no roll is wasted on an awkward gap.

Frequently asked

What happens on doubles? You play the rolled number four times instead of twice.

What is a blot? A lone checker on a point. It can be hit and sent to the bar.

Can you move if you have a checker on the bar? No — you must re-enter it first, and if you cannot, you forfeit the turn.
