Scoring Five Crowns

Five Crowns — often written 5 Crowns — is rummy with a rotating wild card and a five-suit deck — the fifth suit is stars — and it runs exactly eleven rounds. The deal grows from three cards to thirteen, and the wild card grows with it: threes are wild in the three-card round, and by the last round the Kings go wild.

What scores

Cards melded into books and runs score nothing. Everything left in hand counts against you: face value for number cards, 11-12-13 for jack, queen and king, 20 for the current wild card, and 50 for a joker. Lowest total after round eleven wins.

Those last two values shape the whole game — holding a joker hunting for the perfect run is a 50-point gamble every time someone else goes out.

Why the wild column matters

The single most-asked question at a Five Crowns table is "what's wild this round?" — so it's printed on every row of this sheet next to the deal size. When the discard pile answers get slow, check the row, not the rulebook.

Going out

When a player lays down their whole hand, everyone else gets one final turn to meld what they can before counting. That last turn is where jokers either find a home or cost 50.

Frequently asked

How many rounds? Eleven — deals of 3 through 13 cards.

What's wild? Jokers always, plus the card matching the round's deal size: 3s in round one, up through Kings in round eleven.

What's a leftover joker worth? Fifty points against you — the most expensive card in the game.
