Simple rotations — after each round the winning pair at every table moves up one (table 1 stays), and winners split as partners at the new table. Ghost rounds repeat until the party says stop.
SetupHow to run it
2 tables · 8Winners at table 2 move to table 1; losers at table 1 move to table 2. Movers split up; new partners cut for seats.
3 tables · 12Winners move 2 → 1, 3 → 2, and table 1’s winners drop to table 3. Split every moving pair.
4 tables · 16Winners move up one table (1’s winners loop to 4). Split every moving pair; losers keep their seats but change partners.
Score each round however your group likes — Chicago-style bridge scoring is traditional, but many parties just record points or wins. The tally only needs numbers it can add.

Running party bridge

Party (or progressive) bridge turns a partnership game into a social one: play a round at your table — typically four deals — then the room rotates, partners change, and your tally card follows you. By the last round everyone has played with and against most of the room, which is the point. Highest personal total takes the prize.

The rotation, without arguments

The engine printed on the sheet is the classic: winners move up, movers split. It needs no chart lookup and survives any number of rounds. If your club prefers fixed Howell-style movements, run those — the tally cards don't care how the bodies move, only that scores land in rows.

Scoring rounds

Chicago scoring (each round is four deals, vulnerability rotating) is the traditional partner to a tally evening, and our rubber bridge pad prints its point values if a table wants them at hand. Plenty of parties simplify to raw points or even games-won — the card adds anything.

Frequently asked

Both pairs tie a round — who moves? House choice; the common fix is a single extra deal or a high-card cut. Decide before round one and write it somewhere visible.

Odd number of players? Run a sit-out seat that rotates, or draft the host — tally cards make a skipped round obvious and fair.
