Canasta scoring, on one page

Canasta's rules are not complicated, but its scoring has three separate tables — card values, bonuses, and a sliding initial meld requirement — and nobody remembers all three. That is what this sheet is for.

The initial meld

The requirement rises as your team's score does: below zero you need 15, from 0 to 1,495 you need 50, from 1,500 to 2,995 you need 90, and at 3,000 or above you need 120. It is counted from card values only — bonuses do not count toward it.

Canastas

A canasta is seven cards of a rank. A natural one, with no wild cards, is worth 500; a mixed one containing wilds is worth 300. Your team must have at least one canasta before it can go out.

Red threes

Red threes are bonus cards — you never meld them, you just lay them down and draw a replacement. Each is worth 100, and holding all four is worth 800. If your team has not melded by the end of the hand, they count against you instead.

A word on variants

Canasta is played several different ways. American Canasta, Classic Canasta and Hand and Foot all use different meld minimums and bonuses. The values on this sheet are the widely played American rules — if your group does something different, settle it before the first deal rather than in the middle of hand four.
