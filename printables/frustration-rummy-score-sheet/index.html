How to play Frustration Rummy

Frustration is contract rummy at marathon length: fourteen contracts, completed in order, and you cannot move to the next until you've laid down the current one. Players end up rounds apart — which is where the name comes from.

The turn

Deal thirteen from a double deck with jokers. Each turn: draw (stock or discard), meld if you can complete your contract, lay off on the table once you're down, discard. Sets are three-plus of a rank; runs are four-plus in one suit. Wilds — jokers, and deuces where your family plays them wild — may never outnumber the natural cards in a meld.

Scoring and winning

The sheet's columns track both the contract each player is on and their points. When someone goes out, the others count their hands against themselves — the common values are 5 for cards up to 9, 10 for 10-through-king, 15 or 20 for aces and more for wilds, but families differ, so agree first. The first player through contract fourteen wins outright; points break ties and settle bragging rights.

About that contract list

This is the part to say plainly: Frustration is a folk game, and the fourteen contracts printed on this sheet are one widely played list, not scripture. Some families run ten contracts, some twelve, some swap a run of nine for two sets of five. If yours differs, cross out and write over — the sheet's job is ending the "what's round eight again?" argument, whatever your round eight is.

Frequently asked

Can you complete two contracts in one round? No — one contract per round, then you're laying off, not advancing.

What if you fail a contract? You attempt the same one next round while everyone who made theirs moves on.
