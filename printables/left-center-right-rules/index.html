The rules — all of them

  • Everyone starts with 3 chips · roll one die per chip you hold, up to three
  • L — pass a chip left · R — pass a chip right · C — a chip to the center pot (gone for good)
  • Dot — keep a chip, nothing happens
  • No chips? You’re not out — you skip your roll, but chips can be passed back to you
  • When only one player holds chips, they win everything in the pot

How to play Left Center Right

Give everyone three chips — quarters, candy, poker chips, anything worth mildly coveting. On your turn, roll one die per chip you hold (up to three) and obey the faces: L hands a chip to your left neighbor, R to your right, C banishes one to the center pot forever, and a dot lets a chip stay home. That's it. There are no decisions. That's not a flaw — it's the entire product.

Why the "no decisions" game survives every party

Because chips flow in circles, nobody's ever really gone: you can sit chipless for three rounds and inherit a fortune from a neighbor's bad roll. The endgame is genuinely tense — the last two chip-holders trade fate one roll at a time while the table hollers — and the winner takes a pot that's been growing all game. Play with quarters and it's a gambling game; play with wrapped candy and it's a kids' game; the rules never change.

The conversion chart

The branded dice are just d6s in costume: three dot faces, one each of L, C, R. So the chart on the sheet — 1-3 keep, 4 left, 5 center, 6 right — reproduces the real game's probabilities exactly with any dice in the junk drawer. Three dice and a bag of anything, and you're playing.

Frequently asked

How long does a game run? Five to ten minutes with four to six players — which is why the tracker has eight rows. Nobody plays LCR once.

Variants? The common one: the last chip must survive a final roll (a C on the last chip keeps the game alive). Agree before the first toss.
