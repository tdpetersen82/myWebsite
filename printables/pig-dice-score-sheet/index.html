Rules & the famous variant

  • On your turn, roll as many times as you dare — each roll adds to your turn total
  • Roll a 1 — the turn total vanishes and the die passes
  • Hold — bank the turn total onto your score, then pass the die
  • First to 100 wins (agree whether exactly or over)
  • Two-Dice Pig: roll both — a single 1 ends the turn; double 1s wipe your entire banked score (common version — agree first)

How to play Pig

One die circles the table. On your turn, roll: a 2 through 6 adds to your turn total, and you choose — roll again, or hold and bank the total onto your score. Roll a 1 and the turn total evaporates, die passes, table laughs. First to 100 wins. A six-year-old learns it in one round; a statistician can spend a career on it.

The decision that teaches probability

Every roll risks one-sixth of a disaster against five-sixths of progress, which makes "when do I stop?" a genuine expected-value problem — the reason Pig appears in math classrooms everywhere. The folk answer, hold at 20, falls out of the arithmetic, but the game's charm is that the arithmetic stops mattering when you're 8 points behind with the die in your hand.

Turning up the heat

Two-Dice Pig doubles the speed and the terror: both dice count, a single 1 ends the turn, and snake eyes torch your whole banked score. It turns a gentle classroom game into a proper gambling story — the sheet's second grid is usually where that version gets played. Graduated from Pig? 10,000 and Farkle are this game with more dice and more ways to be sorry.

Frequently asked

Exactly 100 or over? Either is played; over-the-line is the common default. The pad's caption says agree — so agree.

Does everyone get equal turns? House choice: sudden-death at 100, or finish the round so everyone has rolled equally. The second is fairer; the first is funnier.
