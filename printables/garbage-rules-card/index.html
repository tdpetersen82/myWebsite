The rules

  • Draw from the stock or take the top discard
  • A–10: put it face up in its slot, pick up the card that was there, keep going
  • Jack = wild — drop it in any empty slot (a later exact card may swap it out)
  • Queen or King = garbage — discard it, turn over
  • A card whose slot is already filled is garbage too
  • First to fill all ten slots wins the round — everyone else gets no extra turns
  • Round winner plays the next round with 9 slots, then 8, 7… — first to win the one-card round (an Ace!) wins the game

House variants — circle what your table plays

  • Kings wild instead of Jacks (then Jacks and Queens are garbage) — common in families, not the standard
  • Jokers in, double-wild — wilds anywhere, swappable
  • Everyone finishes the deal: when someone fills their last slot, players to dealer’s right get one final turn
  • Everyone shrinks: all players drop a card each round, not just the round winner
Garbage — also called Trash — is a public-domain children’s game; the standard rules above follow the widely published version (wild Jacks, dead Queens and Kings). The variant box exists because no two households agree.

How to play Garbage

Garbage is the perfect first card game: the whole strategy is knowing your numbers and the whole drama is flipping a card you desperately need face up out of your own grid. Ten face-down cards stand for Ace through 10. Draw, place, flip what was underneath, place again — a good chain fills half the board in one turn, and a Queen ends it mid-gloat.

The chain is the game

Placing a 4 turns up whatever was hiding in the 4-slot. If that's a 7, it goes to the 7-slot and turns up another — the chain keeps rolling until you hit garbage (a Queen, a King, or a rank you've already filled). Ending a turn by choosing the discard instead of the blind stock is the one real decision, and kids find it fast.

Winning and shrinking

Fill all ten and the round ends on the spot. Your reward is a harder game: nine slots next round, then eight, down to a single slot that only an Ace or a wild can fill — the tensest single card flip in children's card gaming. A full game runs twenty minutes; nobody has ever agreed to stop at one game. It sits right beside Crazy Eights and Go Fish in the learn-to-play canon — both playable on this site when the deck's lost under the couch.

Frequently asked

Can you swap a wild out later? Standard: yes — draw the exact card a Jack is holding for, swap it in, and the freed Jack immediately fills any other empty slot. That rule is most of the late-round comebacks.

What if the stock runs out? Shuffle the discards (minus the top card) into a new stock and keep going. With the right deck count it rarely happens twice.
