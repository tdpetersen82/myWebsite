The rules that decide the money

How to read this chart

Three Card Poker is really two games at one table. The ante and play wager is you against the dealer. Pair Plus is a separate bet on your own three cards that ignores the dealer entirely. You can place either or both.

The hand ranking is not five-card poker

The single most important line on this card: a straight beats a flush. With only three cards, straights are harder to make than flushes, so the order is inverted from the game you already know. Players who bring five-card instincts to the table misread their hand and fold winners.

The dealer qualification rule

The dealer only qualifies with queen high or better. If the dealer does not qualify, your ante pays 1 to 1 and your play bet is returned — you cannot win the play bet, no matter how strong your hand. That is why an enormous hand against a non-qualifying dealer feels so flat.

Basic strategy

The optimal rule is short enough to memorise: raise with Q-6-4 or better, fold everything else. That is, if your highest card is a queen, look at the second card — if it is a 7 or higher, raise; if it is a 6, raise only when your third card is a 4 or higher. Anything king-high or better is an automatic raise. Following it costs the house edge roughly 3.4% of the ante; deviating costs more.

A note on pay tables

Pay tables differ between casinos, and the Pair Plus flush and three-of-a-kind rows are the ones that move. The chart shows the two most common versions — check the felt before you sit down, because the difference between 3-to-1 and 4-to-1 on a flush is a meaningful swing in the house edge.
