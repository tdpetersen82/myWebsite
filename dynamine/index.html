How to play Dynamine

Dynamine is a maze bomber in the Bomberman tradition, set in a limestone mine. The galleries are choked with rock, and the only way through is dynamite. Lay a bundle, step clear, and two seconds later it goes off in a cross of fire that clears the rock and anything else it touches — bats, knockers, other miners, and you if you linger. Blast through rock to find gear. The open cyan floor shafts are connected: walk onto one and place dynamite on it. When the fuse burns down, fire bursts from every shaft, reaching one tile in each direction. Shafts stay usable after the blast. Clear the remaining creatures, then walk into the green EXIT mineshaft to finish the level.

Controls

  • ← ↑ → ↓ or W A S D — move
  • Space — lay dynamite (also Z, X, Enter)
  • F — use your equipped power: fireball or ignitor. Powers last 40 seconds; picking up another replaces the current power.
  • P — pause
  • Two players: the blue miner uses WASD + Space, the red miner uses the arrow keys + Enter. Powers: blue F, red Right Shift.
  • On a phone, use the on-screen pad, dynamite button, and POWER button.

Modes

  • Adventure. Solo levels that get denser and meaner. Every level has visible cyan floor shafts. Place dynamite ON any shaft to blast every shaft at once, with a one-tile range around each. Clear all creatures to turn the visible EXIT green, then walk across it. An orange exit is locked until those creatures are defeated. Three lives, a three-minute clock per level, and a score that saves as your best. Run the clock out and the gas catches: sparks come hunting.
  • Battle the computer. Two miners, one gallery with connected shafts, last one standing takes the round. First to three rounds wins the match. With 45 seconds left the roof starts caving in from the edges, so a stand-off never lasts.
  • Two players. The same gallery, two people on one keyboard.

Gear

  • 🔥 Fireball flask — press F to shoot in the direction you face. Stops at rock and walls, defeats creatures, and ignites bombs. Reusable for 40 seconds.
  • 🟣 Ignitor — press F to detonate your oldest dynamite early. Works through connected shafts. Bombs still explode normally after two seconds.
  • 🧨 Dynamite — lay one more bundle at a time (up to six).
  • 💥 Blast — the explosion reaches one tile further in every direction.
  • 👢 Boots — move faster. Stacks three times.
  • ⛑ Helmet — survive one hit.

In Adventure permanent gear carries over between levels; temporary powers reset on a new level or after losing a life. In Battle everyone starts each round fresh, and the gallery hides a full set to race for.

Tips & strategy

  • Never blast in a dead end. Before you light it, know which way you're leaving. A one-tile pocket with dynamite at your back is where most runs end.
  • Dynamite is a wall. Once you step off a bundle you can't walk back over it, and neither can a critter. Use one to plug a corridor between you and something nasty.
  • Corners are safety. Fire only travels in straight lines, so a step around a pillar puts you out of reach of any blast.
  • Chain them. A blast that reaches another bundle sets it off at once. Two bundles with a long fuse can be turned into one wide trap.
  • Rock shields you. The blast stops at the first rock it hits, so an unbroken boulder between you and the dynamite is as good as a wall.
  • Hunt the hunters. Knockers, the green mine goblins, chase when they see you down a corridor. Let one line up on you, lay a bundle, and step around the corner.
  • Use the connected shafts. Place dynamite ON a cyan shaft to fire through all of them. Every shaft flashes while the fuse burns. Keep clear of every shaft and its four neighboring tiles. Walls and rocks block the blast as usual; shaft blasts always have a one-tile range, regardless of your range upgrades.
  • In Battle, take the middle. The cave-in eats the edges first. Whoever holds the centre with more blast range usually wins the endgame.

About Dynamine

The maze-bomber formula — a grid of pillars, breakable blocks, bombs on a fuse and enemies that wander in — dates to Hudson Soft's Bomberman in 1983 and its four-player battle mode on the SNES a decade later. Dynamine is our own take, moved underground to a limestone mine: the same one-more-go loop, drawn fresh with dynamite, boulders, bats and knockers, and a computer opponent that knows better than to stand in its own blast.

Frequently asked
